# Related Work — positioning PaRC

> **Scope note.** This section positions PaRC against prior art; it is results-independent (it does not assert PaRC's empirical wins, which are gated by the pre-registered M0 kill-gate — see Method). Claims here are about *where the method sits*, not *that it succeeds*. Consistent with `method_parc.md`.

## Pointwise LLM relevance scoring
A frozen LLM can score each candidate's absolute relevance `P(relevant | history, candidate)` and rank by that pointwise posterior (the lane of the sibling pointwise-posterior reranker we anchor on). Such a scorer is, by construction, blind to **between-candidate** structure: it never compares two candidates head-to-head. PaRC takes this pointwise posterior as a frozen **anchor** and adds only the comparative correction `β` that a forced pairwise comparison reveals — `score_i = pony_i + λ·β_i`, with `λ ≥ 0` validation-selected and `λ = 0` recovering the pointwise order exactly. We claim the residual is not captured by **this** pointwise posterior under the **same** frozen LLM and prompt family — deliberately *not* the stronger and unprovable "absent from any conceivable pointwise scorer."

## LLM rerankers (pairwise / listwise), incl. RankGPT-style
A large body of work prompts an LLM to rerank candidates directly — pointwise, pairwise, or listwise: RankGPT-style sliding-window listwise ranking \citep{sun2023rankgpt} and Pairwise Ranking Prompting \citep{qin2024prp} (both IR/document ranking), and the recommendation-specific line of LLM rankers/rerankers \citep{hou2024llmrank,gao2024llm4rerank}. **The sharpest objection is that PaRC is "PRP-Sorting plus Bradley–Terry/Elo smoothing plus base-ranker interpolation"** — PRP already uses *symmetrized* pairwise LLM comparisons, an `O(N log N)` sort, and a fallback to the initial order. We rebut this on the controlled object, not the mechanism (the pairwise-comparison machinery is indeed shared): PRP and its active/sorting variants **output a ranking** by aggregating wins; PaRC instead estimates a **comparative residual `β` over a *fixed* pointwise posterior** and never produces a standalone ranking. **β-identifiability (what makes the "residual" real, not a reparameterization of pony):** in `θ_i = α·pony_i + β_i` the scalar `α` absorbs the entire pony-aligned (collinear) component of the BT-fitted log-strengths, and `β` is **ridge-regularized** (L2 penalty, mean-centered) so it departs from `0` only where the duels consistently demand it — `β` is thus the regularized comparative component that is *not* a rescaling of the pointwise posterior; it is identified up to the usual BT shift/scale, fixed by centering and the `α`-projection (see Method §3 for the exact ridge least-squares objective and its sparse-graph role). Whether that residual carries *ranking* signal is then an **empirical, pre-registered M0 question** (does `λ>0` lift NDCG beyond `λ=0`?), not an assumption. PaRC further differs by being **compute-bounded** (`O(K log K)` adaptive duels, short ~300–600-token symmetrized prompts, vLLM-batchable) where listwise panels scale poorly; we compute-match RankGPT/PRP-style rerankers so a win cannot be attributed merely to spending more LLM calls. **On the floor:** because `λ=0` lies in the validation search space, the selected mixture **cannot underperform the pony anchor *on validation* by construction; test non-degradation is not guaranteed** by finite validation selection — we therefore claim only non-inferiority within a pre-set margin `ε` on test (and kill the method if `λ` collapses to ~0), not guaranteed test dominance.

## Bradley–Terry models and pairwise preference aggregation
Bradley–Terry (BT) \citep{bradley1952rank}, Thurstone \citep{thurstone1927law}, Plackett–Luce \citep{plackett1975permutations,luce1959choice}, and Elo \citep{elo1978rating} models recover latent item strengths from pairwise/listwise comparisons and are classical in ranking-from-comparisons. PaRC's novelty is not BT estimation per se but the **anchored decomposition** `θ_i = α·pony_i + β_i`, which separates the part of the comparative order already explained by the pointwise posterior (`α·pony`) from the genuinely new comparative residual (`β`). On top of this we add (i) **symmetrization** `s_ij = ½(logit(A,B) − logit(B,A))` to cancel the LLM's position bias (with the discarded antisymmetric part retained as a bias diagnostic), and (ii) a test for **non-BT cyclic intransitivity**: if the symmetrized preference field exhibits statistically significant cyclic structure beyond a BT + heteroskedastic-noise null, then — since any *scalar* utility model with a monotone pairwise-choice link (BT/Thurstone/Plackett–Luce) induces stochastic transitivity and so **cannot represent significant cyclic pairwise marginals** \citep{tversky1969intransitivity} — no *such scalar-utility* pointwise model reproduces the order (we scope the statement to scalar-utility models with a monotone link, not as a universal impossibility). This is a scientific sub-finding about LLM pairwise judgments, claimed only where the cyclicity is significant and predictive of the lift (never assumed). We avoid over-formalizing it as a "representation theorem": the operative statement is the transitivity property of scalar-utility models, not a named theorem.

## LLM-as-judge and pairwise comparison signals
Using LLMs as pairwise judges is well established (preference modeling, RLHF data, evaluation) \citep{zheng2023judging}, and LLM pairwise judgments are documented to be order- and context-sensitive \citep{qin2024prp,zheng2023judging}. We distinguish carefully: that work establishes *position/verbosity/self-enhancement bias* (order-sensitivity), which our symmetrization targets — it is **not** evidence of *cyclic intransitivity*. The latter is a separate, stronger property (a violation of stochastic transitivity \citep{tversky1969intransitivity}) that PaRC tests for explicitly and claims only where significant, rather than importing from the order-sensitivity literature. PaRC repurposes forced pairwise comparison as a *recommendation ranking* signal source, and treats the known position/intransitivity pathologies head-on — symmetrizing to remove position bias and measuring intransitivity as a diagnostic rather than ignoring it.

## Efficiency of LLM-based reranking
Listwise LLM rerankers that present a large candidate panel in one prompt (the design of our own superseded predecessor, CC-PACE) are slow and batch-unfriendly: one long prompt per user drives effective batch size toward one and forfeits continuous batching, and the long context diffuses per-candidate signal. PaRC's short, symmetrized, `O(K log K)` duels are individually cheap and vLLM-batchable, targeting fewer total tokens than a listwise panel while concentrating comparisons at the top-`k` boundary where ranking errors cost. Efficiency is reported on a compute-normalized axis (lift per 1k prompts / per GPU-hour) so the contribution is not an "expensive calibration layer."
